5. Theory

A Boost Converter is a DC-DC converter that produces an output voltage greater than the input voltage. The converter consists of an inductor, controlled switch, diode, capacitor and load.

Continuous Conduction Mode

For an ideal Boost Converter operating in continuous conduction mode:

Vo = Vin / (1 − D)
D = 1 − (Vin / Vo)
Io = Vo / R
Po = Vo × Io
Pin = Vin × Iin
η = (Po / Pin) × 100
Important: The above voltage conversion equation is for an ideal Boost Converter operating in continuous conduction mode. Practical Simulink results may differ because of switching losses, diode/MOSFET losses, inductor resistance, capacitor ESR and discontinuous conduction.

8. Procedure

  1. Open MATLAB and Simulink.
  2. Create a new Simulink model.
  3. Connect the DC source to the input of the Boost Converter.
  4. Connect the inductor between the source and switching node.
  5. Connect the controlled MOSFET/IGBT from the switching node to ground.
  6. Connect the diode between the switching node and output.
  7. Connect the capacitor across the output load.
  8. Apply PWM switching pulses to the controlled switch.
  9. Set the required input voltage and duty ratio.
  10. Measure output voltage and output current.
  11. Connect the measurements to Scope blocks.
  12. Run the simulation.
  13. Apply the variable load profile given below.
  14. Record output voltage, current, power and efficiency.
  15. Observe the transient response during each load change.

13. Sample Calculation

For an ideal Boost Converter:

Vo = Vin / (1 − D)

Example: Vin = 24 V and D = 0.5

Vo = 24 / (1 − 0.5)
Vo = 48 V

If R = 20 Ω:

Io = 48 / 20 = 2.4 A
Po = 48 × 2.4 = 115.2 W
