Experiment 2C – Buck-Boost Converter with Variable Load Profile

1. Student Details

2. Aim

To model and simulate an inverting buck-boost DC–DC converter in MATLAB/Simulink and study output voltage, current, power, efficiency, and transient response for different duty ratios and load resistances.

3. Learning Objectives

  • Understand buck-boost converter operation during switch ON and OFF states.
  • Relate duty ratio to the magnitude and polarity of output voltage.
  • Build a switching converter model in MATLAB/Simulink.
  • Observe output response under duty-ratio and load changes.
  • Calculate output power and conversion efficiency from measured values.

4. Software / Simulink Blocks Required

  • MATLAB and Simulink
  • Simscape Electrical or Specialized Power Systems (use one library consistently)
  • DC Voltage Source, Inductor, MOSFET/Ideal Switch, Diode, Capacitor, Resistor
  • PWM/Pulse Generator, Voltage and Current Measurement
  • Scope, powergui (for Specialized Power Systems models)

5. Theory and Equations

The conventional inverting buck-boost converter can produce an output magnitude below or above the input voltage. Its output polarity is opposite to the input reference.

Ideal CCM equations

Vₒ = − [D / (1 − D)] × Vᵢₙ
|Vₒ| = [D / (1 − D)] × Vᵢₙ
D = |Vₒ| / (Vᵢₙ + |Vₒ|)
Iₒ = |Vₒ| / R
Pₒ = |Vₒ| × Iₒ
Pᵢₙ = Vᵢₙ × Iᵢₙ
η = (Pₒ / Pᵢₙ) × 100%
Important: These equations assume an ideal inverting buck-boost converter in continuous conduction mode (CCM). Actual output voltage and efficiency depend on switch/diode losses, inductor resistance, capacitor ESR, switching frequency, load, and whether the converter enters discontinuous conduction mode (DCM). For calculations below, output voltage is displayed as a negative value; output current and power are shown as magnitudes.

8. Procedure

  1. Open MATLAB/Simulink and create a new model.
  2. Select one electrical modelling technology and add the required source, inductor, switch, diode, capacitor, and load.
  3. Wire the inverting buck-boost topology carefully and verify diode orientation.
  4. Drive the MOSFET/ideal switch using a PWM pulse train. Set the switching frequency and duty ratio.
  5. Measure output voltage with polarity consistent with the negative output; also measure output current and input current.
  6. Set solver and electrical network settings appropriate to the selected library.
  7. Run the model and inspect switching waveforms and steady-state output.
  8. Change duty ratio and load resistance, then record measured results.
  9. Compare measured results with the ideal CCM equations and explain differences.

12. Sample Calculation

For Vᵢₙ = 24 V and D = 0.5:

Vₒ = −[0.5 / (1 − 0.5)] × 24 = −24 V

For R = 20 Ω, output current magnitude and power are:

Iₒ = |−24| / 20 = 1.2 A
Pₒ = 24 × 1.2 = 28.8 W

This is an ideal CCM calculation. It is not a prediction of startup transients or a guarantee that the selected L, C, frequency, and load maintain CCM.

15. Post-Lab Questions

  1. Explain the operation of an inverting buck-boost converter during switch ON and OFF states.
  2. Derive the ideal CCM voltage conversion ratio.
  3. Why is the output voltage polarity inverted?
  4. What duty ratio gives an output-voltage magnitude equal to the input voltage in the ideal model?
  5. What is the function of the inductor and capacitor?
  6. How do load resistance and duty ratio affect output current?
  7. What is the difference between CCM and DCM?
  8. Why can the practical output differ from the ideal equation?
  9. Identify the main sources of losses.
  10. How can output-voltage ripple be reduced?
