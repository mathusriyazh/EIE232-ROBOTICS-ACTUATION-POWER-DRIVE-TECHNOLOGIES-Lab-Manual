5. Theory

A Flyback Converter is an isolated DC-DC converter derived from the buck-boost converter. Electrical isolation between the input and output is obtained using a high-frequency transformer or coupled inductor.

The Flyback Converter stores energy in the magnetizing inductance during the switch ON interval. When the switch is turned OFF, the stored energy is transferred to the secondary side through the transformer and diode.

Basic Operating Principle

Switch ON: The primary switch conducts and the input voltage is applied to the primary winding. Magnetizing current increases and energy is stored in the magnetizing inductance.

Switch OFF: The primary switch is turned OFF. The polarity of the transformer windings reverses and the secondary diode conducts. Stored magnetic energy is transferred to the output capacitor and load.

Ideal Flyback Voltage Relationship

For an ideal Flyback Converter operating in continuous conduction mode, the magnitude of the output voltage can be approximated by:

|Vo| = (Ns/Np) × D/(1-D) × Vin

where:

  • Np = Primary turns
  • Ns = Secondary turns
  • D = Duty ratio
  • Vin = Input voltage
Turns Ratio n = Ns/Np
Vo = n × D/(1-D) × Vin
Io = Vo/R
Po = Vo × Io
Pin = Vin × Iin
η = (Po/Pin) × 100
Note: The above equation is an idealized relationship. The actual MATLAB/Simulink output may differ because of winding resistance, leakage inductance, switching losses, diode forward voltage, transformer losses, capacitor ESR and other non-ideal effects.

8. Procedure

  1. Open MATLAB and Simulink.
  2. Create a new Simulink model.
  3. Place a DC voltage source at the input.
  4. Use a high-frequency transformer or coupled inductor as the isolating magnetic component.
  5. Connect a MOSFET/IGBT as the primary-side switching device.
  6. Connect a diode on the secondary side.
  7. Connect an output capacitor across the load.
  8. Connect the resistive load at the output.
  9. Generate PWM pulses for the primary-side switch.
  10. Set the required switching frequency and duty ratio.
  11. Measure primary current, secondary current and output voltage.
  12. Connect the measurements to Scope blocks.
  13. Run the simulation.
  14. Apply the variable load profile.
  15. Record output voltage, current, power and efficiency.
  16. Observe the transient response when the load changes.
