5. Theory

A bidirectional DC-DC converter allows electrical power to flow in both directions between a DC bus and a battery.

Charging Mode – Buck Operation

During charging, power flows from the DC bus to the battery. The converter operates approximately as a buck converter.

Vbatt ≈ D × Vdc

The battery receives positive charging current.

Discharging Mode – Boost Operation

During discharging, power flows from the battery to the DC bus. The converter operates approximately as a boost converter.

Vdc ≈ Vbatt/(1-D)

The battery supplies current to the DC bus.

Power Calculation

Pbatt = Vbatt × Ibatt
Pdc = Vdc × Idc
η = Pout/Pin × 100

Battery State of Charge

An approximate SOC calculation can be obtained using the current integration method:

SOC(t) = SOC(t0) + (1/Qrated) ∫ Ibatt dt

The sign convention must be clearly defined. In this experiment, positive battery current represents charging current.

Important: Actual battery charging should normally use a suitable CC-CV charging strategy and appropriate battery-management protections. The Simulink experiment is intended to study converter operation and control.

8. Operating Modes

Charging: DC Bus → Converter → Battery

9. Procedure

  1. Open MATLAB and Simulink.
  2. Create a new Simulink model.
  3. Place the DC bus source and battery model.
  4. Insert the bidirectional DC-DC converter between the DC bus and battery.
  5. Use two controlled switches/MOSFETs for bidirectional operation.
  6. Connect the inductor in the converter power path.
  7. Generate PWM signals for both operating directions.
  8. Set the battery initial SOC.
  9. Run the converter in charging mode.
  10. Measure battery voltage and battery current.
  11. Measure DC bus voltage and current.
  12. Calculate charging power and converter efficiency.
  13. Repeat the experiment in discharging mode.
  14. Observe the reversal of power flow.
  15. Apply the variable charging/discharging profile.

15. Sample Calculation

Charging Mode

Assume:

  • Vdc = 48 V
  • D = 0.5
Vbatt ≈ D × Vdc
Vbatt ≈ 0.5 × 48 = 24 V

Charging Power

If:

Vbatt = 24 V
Ibatt = 5 A
# Pbatt # 24 × 5 120 W

Discharging Mode

For a battery voltage of 24 V and duty ratio D = 0.5:

Vdc ≈ Vbatt/(1-D)
Vdc ≈ 24/(1-0.5) = 48 V

Efficiency

η = Pout/Pin × 100
