5. Theory

An H-Bridge DC motor drive consists of four controlled switches arranged in an H configuration. The motor is connected between the two bridge legs.

By controlling the switching devices using PWM signals, the average voltage applied to the motor can be controlled.

PWM Principle

Duty Ratio D = Ton / T
PWM Frequency fs = 1 / T

For an ideal unipolar/bipolar PWM DC drive, the average motor voltage can be approximated as:

Vavg ≈ D × Vdc

where:

  • D = duty ratio
  • Vdc = DC-link voltage
  • Vavg = average motor voltage

Motor Relations

Va = Eb + IaRa
Eb = Keω
Te = KtIa
Tm = Te - TL
ω = 2πN / 60

Electrical input power:

Pin = Va × Ia

Mechanical output power:

Pmech = Te × ω

Approximate drive efficiency:

η = (Pmech / Pin) × 100
Important: Actual motor-drive results will differ from ideal equations because of switching losses, motor resistance, inductance, back EMF, semiconductor voltage drops, friction, mechanical inertia and load torque.

8. Motor Direction Control

For an H-Bridge, changing the appropriate diagonal switch pair changes the direction of motor voltage and hence the direction of rotation. Never turn ON both switches in the same bridge leg simultaneously. Use appropriate dead time/interlocking in the practical circuit.

9. Procedure

  1. Open MATLAB and Simulink.
  2. Create a new Simulink model.
  3. Place a DC voltage source.
  4. Connect an H-Bridge/Universal Bridge to the DC source.
  5. Connect a separately excited or permanent-magnet DC motor to the H-Bridge.
  6. Connect the mechanical load to the motor shaft.
  7. Generate PWM pulses using a PWM Generator.
  8. Apply complementary gate signals to the appropriate bridge switches.
  9. Set the PWM frequency and duty ratio.
  10. Run the simulation.
  11. Observe motor voltage, armature current, speed and electromagnetic torque.
  12. Change the duty ratio and repeat the simulation.
  13. Apply forward and reverse switching and observe motor direction.
  14. Change the load torque and study the transient response.
  15. Record the measured values in the observation table.
