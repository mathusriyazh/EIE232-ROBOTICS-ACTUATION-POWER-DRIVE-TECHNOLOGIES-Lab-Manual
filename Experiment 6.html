Experiment 6 – Speed Control of BLDC Motor Drive

1. Student Details

2. Aim

To simulate and study the speed control of a Brushless DC (BLDC) motor drive using PWM control and electronic commutation, and to analyze the effect of PWM duty ratio and load torque on motor speed, current and electromagnetic torque.

3. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the construction and operating principle of a BLDC motor.
  • Understand electronic commutation using Hall-effect position sensors.
  • Understand six-step inverter operation.
  • Generate PWM signals for BLDC speed control.
  • Study the relationship between PWM duty ratio and motor speed.
  • Analyze the effect of load torque on speed and current.
  • Observe phase currents, Hall signals, speed and electromagnetic torque.
  • Understand closed-loop speed control of a BLDC motor.

5. Theory

A Brushless DC motor is a permanent-magnet synchronous motor in which mechanical commutation using brushes and a commutator is replaced by electronic commutation.

The rotor position is generally detected using Hall-effect sensors. The Hall signals are used to select the appropriate inverter switches.

Six-Step Electronic Commutation

Hall A Hall B Hall C Typical Energized Phases
101A+ , B−
100A+ , C−
110B+ , C−
010B+ , A−
011C+ , A−
001C+ , B−

The exact Hall sequence and switch assignment depend on the motor phase sequence and inverter convention used in the Simulink model.

PWM Speed Control

D = Ton / T
fs = 1 / T

Increasing the PWM duty ratio increases the average voltage applied to the motor phases and generally increases motor speed, subject to back-EMF, load torque and motor-drive losses.

BLDC Motor Equations

Eb = Ke × ω
Te = Kt × I
ω = 2πN / 60
Pmech = Te × ω
Pin = Vdc × Idc
η = (Pmech / Pin) × 100
Important: The relationship between duty ratio and speed is not perfectly linear in a practical BLDC drive because speed also depends on back EMF, load torque, winding resistance, inductance, commutation, controller action and losses.

8. Speed Control Mode

In open-loop control, the duty ratio is directly specified. In closed-loop speed control, measured motor speed is compared with the reference speed and the controller adjusts the PWM duty ratio.

10. Procedure

  1. Open MATLAB and Simulink.
  2. Create a new BLDC motor drive model.
  3. Connect a DC voltage source to a three-phase inverter.
  4. Connect the three-phase inverter to the BLDC motor.
  5. Connect a mechanical load to the motor shaft.
  6. Use Hall sensors to obtain rotor position information.
  7. Connect Hall signals to the six-step commutation logic.
  8. Generate PWM pulses for controlling the inverter switches.
  9. Set the PWM frequency and initial duty ratio.
  10. Run the simulation.
  11. Observe Hall signals, gate pulses, phase currents, speed and torque.
  12. Increase the PWM duty ratio and repeat the simulation.
  13. Apply different load torques and observe the speed response.
  14. Implement closed-loop speed control using a PI controller.
  15. Compare reference speed and actual motor speed.
  16. Record the observations in the tables.
