2. Aim

6. Theory

6.1 Synchronous Speed

Ns = 120 f / P

where:
Ns = synchronous speed in rpm
f = supply frequency in Hz
P = number of poles

6.2 Rotor Speed

N = Ns(1 - s)

where s is the slip.

6.3 Slip

s = (Ns - N) / Ns
Slip (%) = [(Ns - N) / Ns] × 100

6.4 Frequency Control

The most commonly used method for high-performance induction motor speed control is variable-frequency control using a VFD.

Speed ≈ 120f/P × (1-s)

6.5 Constant V/f Control

Below the rated frequency, the VFD approximately maintains the voltage-to-frequency ratio constant.

V/f ≈ Constant

Therefore, if the rated point is 415 V at 50 Hz, the idealized voltage reference at 25 Hz is approximately:

V25 ≈ 415 × 25/50 = 207.5 V
Actual VFD output voltage may differ because of motor parameters, boost settings, PWM modulation, inverter losses and the specific VFD control algorithm.

6.6 Motor Input Power

Pin = √3 VL IL cosφ

6.7 Mechanical Output Power

Pout = T × ω
ω = 2πN / 60

6.8 Efficiency

η = Pout / Pin × 100

6.9 Torque

T = Pout / ω

9. VFD Parameter Settings

Before running the experiment, enter the motor nameplate values into the VFD according to the manufacturer's parameter structure. Typical values include:
  • Motor rated voltage
  • Motor rated current
  • Motor rated frequency
  • Motor rated power
  • Motor rated speed
  • Acceleration time
  • Deceleration time
  • Minimum and maximum frequency

10. Experimental Procedure

  1. Inspect the induction motor, VFD, cables and protection devices.
  2. Record the motor nameplate data.
  3. Connect the three-phase supply to the VFD input through the laboratory protection system.
  4. Connect the VFD output terminals to the motor according to the VFD manufacturer's instructions.
  5. Connect the tachometer or encoder for speed measurement.
  6. Connect the power analyzer / meters required for voltage, current and power measurement.
  7. Enter the motor nameplate parameters into the VFD.
  8. Select V/f or the prescribed laboratory control mode.
  9. Set the acceleration and deceleration times.
  10. Start the motor at a low frequency.
  11. Increase the frequency gradually.
  12. Record motor speed at each frequency.
  13. Record voltage, current and input power.
  14. Repeat the experiment for different load torques.
  15. Calculate synchronous speed and slip.
  16. Calculate mechanical output power when torque is available.
  17. Calculate efficiency.
  18. Observe the motor current and speed during acceleration and deceleration.
  19. Stop the motor using the prescribed VFD stop command.
Never use an abrupt mechanical loading or braking procedure beyond the rating of the motor, dynamometer or VFD.

18. Student Analysis

19. Result

Suggested Result:

The squirrel-cage induction motor speed was successfully controlled by varying the VFD output frequency. The measured speed increased with frequency and remained below synchronous speed because of slip. The effects of load torque, current, V/f ratio and acceleration/deceleration settings were experimentally observed.
